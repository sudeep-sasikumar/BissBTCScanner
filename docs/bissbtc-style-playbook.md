## 1. Core philosophy (what drives every decision)

1. **You're paid to wait, then you hit it hard.** He spends days or weeks doing nothing, then sizes up aggressively when his setup appears. "You get paid to do nothing for ages, then paid massively when the time's right by aping hard and taking a massive amount of risk." (S3, S4)
2. **Buy low, sell high. Trade only the extremes, never the middle.** Mid-range entries mean "my downside is the whole range", so he waits. (S2, S3, S5, S8, S14)
3. **Short rallies, buy dips.** In an uptrend he buys pullbacks. He shorts only when a short is "blaringly obvious". (S3, S5)
4. **Wait for other people to get trapped, then take their money.** His whole edge is market-participant psychology: late longs, stopped-out shorts, breakout chasers. "My system is: we just wait for people to get trapped." (S3, S9, S11)
5. **"I don't catch falling knives."** He lets the low get swept, then enters on the reclaim. (S4, S13)
6. **Protect capital first.** "Number one rule is to not lose money; if you don't lose money you make money by default." On the public challenge he deliberately trades more conservatively than he would on his own account. (S2, S8, S10)
7. **He doesn't care about being right, only about making money.** Bull or bear labels, Twitter calls and news are noise. "The chart doesn't lie, the news does." (S10, S12)
8. **Strong opinions, loosely held.** He plans scenarios in advance but skips a planned trade if the price action into the level looks wrong. (S4, S7, S12)
9. **No forced trades.** The challenge only "starts" with the first trade, which came 9 days in. He took 0 trades in the first 6 streams. (S1–S6)
10. **Stay fresh, like an athlete.** He skips small scalps so he has energy for the big trending moves where most of the money is made. (S6, S7)
11. **Think in probabilities and stack confluences.** A 50/50 setup means no trade. A setup with more reasons gets more size. (S3, S5)

## 2. What he trades (universe and coin selection)

12. **Exchange:** Blofin, USDT-margined perpetuals (USDT is more liquid than USDC), **isolated margin**. He charts on TradingView, usually on the Binance, Coinbase or Bybit feed, because it has the longest history. (S1, S2)
13. **Challenge universe narrowed to BTC, ETH and HYPE only.** BTC is the main one. HYPE can be swapped out if it trades badly. SOL is avoided because it "trades poorly: big dump, sideways, big pump". Reason: maximum size, efficient price action and liquidity. "Bitcoin is always the king." (S12, S13, S14)
14. **Small caps (CASHCAT, BLESS, VVV, PNS and the like) only:** (a) as small "buffer builder" trades posted in the free VIP group, or (b) as spot or 2x once the account is around $10k–$50k. "Alts are leveraged Bitcoin." With under $10k you get more from leverage on a liquid major than from a 50% move on a small cap. (S3, S10, S11, S12)
15. **How he finds coins:** exchange futures list sorted by **24h volume**, plus CoinGecko trending. He wants volatile **and** liquid coins. He avoids dead, old coins (ADA, NEAR, UNI, XRP) and illiquid "turds" that broke key support. "Price is the narrative." (S1, S2, S4)
16. **Relative strength:** he uses ETH/BTC, ETH/SOL and HYPE/BTC to pick the stronger asset. He thinks alts lagging BTC is healthy. If alts lead, it's "building a house on sand". **BNB is his gauge of overall market strength.** (S1, S6, S10, S12)
17. **One position at a time.** He takes a second position only after the first is at breakeven. He never holds conflicting trades (no short BTC plus long ETH). (S6, S11, S14)

## 3. How he analyses a chart

18. **Top-down: HTF analysis, then LTF execution.** He starts on monthly, 2-week, weekly and 3-day charts to set direction and levels, uses daily and 4h for structure, and goes down to 15m, 5m, 3m or 1m to enter. He also does "reverse top-down", reading the 2W, monthly, 3-month and yearly candle closes. (S1, S2, S6, S14)
19. **No indicators.** He uses plain horizontal support and resistance only. Order blocks and FVGs are "just support and resistance repackaged". Diagonals are subjective; he uses trendlines only during a trend. He doesn't use log charts. (S1, S3, S7)
20. **His rule for a good S/R level:** it moved price away in both **time and distance**. He validates a level the same way every time it's retested, and a fake-out above or below doesn't cancel it if price gets back inside. (S2, V1)
21. **Market structure first:** what's being respected and what isn't. HH/HL means an uptrend, LH/LL a downtrend. A support/resistance flip is a core building block. (S1, S11)
22. **Ranges:** after an expansion he marks range high, range low and midpoint. He buys range lows and sells range highs. Triangles are "cursed": they usually fake one way, then go the other, so he treats them as ranges. (S2, S5, S11, S13)
23. **Liquidation heatmap (Coinglass), the main tool.** "On Bitcoin it's all driven by liquidity." He reads the 24h, 48h, 1w, 2w, 1M, 3M and 6M heatmaps and the Coinglass "liquidation levels" model (low, medium and high leverage clusters). He uses the largest clusters as entry zones (long-liquidation clusters below) and targets (short liquidations above). "The bigger the liquidation, the bigger the bounce." He wants one side taken out before committing. (S3, S4, S5, S7, S12, S13)
24. **Volatility regime: BVOL (Bitcoin volatility index, 7-day) plus volume.** Very low volatility and volume means a big expansion candle is coming, and he positions for it. He called "big move soon" on 13 Aug, and it came on 19–20 Aug. (S5, S7, S9)
25. **Timing:**
    - No weekend trading ("a weekend dump leads to a weekday pump, a weekend pump to a dump before the pump"). He doesn't stream Friday to Sunday.
    - The big moves usually come Tuesday to Friday, especially Wednesday and Thursday.
    - Sessions (London, NY, Asia) are "outdated in crypto".
    - Near a quarterly (3-month) candle close he expects chop. Big moves come early or mid-candle.
    - The Monday range is used when it's wide. (S2, S8, S12, S14)
26. **Key reference lines:** the yearly open (BTC 2026 is about 87.6k), weekly and monthly opens, prior all-time highs, and the "last level everyone is watching". (S6, S14, S15)
27. **Impulse cycle:** in his view, BTC trends for about 6–8 weeks, then pulls back or consolidates. After a vertical move the **first pullback is a buy**, because vertical moves almost never reverse straight down. (S10, S14)
28. **Flip the chart.** He inverts the chart and asks "would I short this?" to strip out his bias. (S8)
29. **News is priced in.** It only causes a short-term whipsaw (CPI pumps and dumps both ways, then picks a direction). What matters is how price reacts. (S5, S6, S12)

## 4. The setups he trades (ranked by how much size he gives them)

30. **S-tier ("pristine, god-tier"), max size:** a **flush or sweep of a key HTF low into a liquidation cluster, then a reclaim back above**. Shorts get trapped and late longs get liquidated, then the real move starts. Examples: BTC long under 62k (Aug), 74–75k and later 72–74k (Sep), ETH at 1,800 and then 2,100–2,300. "The bigger the flush, the more size." (S3, S6, S9, S11, S12, S13)
31. **His other "never miss" setup:** **consolidation, then breakout, then retest, then continuation** ("my textbook setup"). (S6)
32. **Range-low long / range-high short** after an expansion. Tier 1 sits just under the obvious lows. Tier 2 sits deeper, at a key daily low or the breakout-retest zone, with more size. (S11, S12, S13)
33. **First pullback after an impulsive breakout** (buying the dip that stops out late longs). (S9, S10)
34. **Breakout trades** (used when the pristine entry never comes): "enter here, cut on a clean break below", with a ruthless stop just under the breakout level. He sizes these smaller, about 5–10%. Breakouts that come **after accumulation** are fine. Breakouts **into resistance after an up-move** are low probability ("you become exit liquidity"). (S8, S9, S10)
35. **Deviation short:** price pokes above key resistance (the yearly open, a range high), then closes back below. He enters on the reclaim below, with the stop above the spike. His first challenge trade (BTC short, Aug) was this setup. (S6, S14)
36. **Fade the first move out of a range.** "If it spikes up first it goes down; if it goes down first it goes up." He used this logic to short the 2021 and 2025 tops. (S3, S11)

## 5. Entries: how he gets in

37. **He plans zones in advance** (green box = buy, red box = sell or TP, drawn with TradingView's long/short position tool). He posts them to the free VIP Telegram, then executes when price arrives. (S2, S8, S15)
38. **He doesn't enter blindly at the level.** He waits for the sweep, then the reclaim or "starting to hold", then enters, often on the 1m–5m chart. "If it just keeps falling through with no bounce, I don't take it." (S7, S8, S11, S13)
39. **Order type changed during the challenge.**
    - Early on he said: "I hate limit orders", "I'll market order everything until the account is $100k–$400k" (S2, S4).
    - In practice, his first trade filled on a limit order while he was on a call (S6).
    - Later he advised followers to put **limit orders at the posted zone**, and to **spread 3–5 limits across the zone with a wider stop**. He himself does about 50% limit and markets the rest (S11, S13).
40. **Uncertain breakout:** he enters **small size first "to feel the market"**, then adds the rest if price pulls back and reclaims. (S8)
41. **Better entry means tighter stop.** He enters as close to the invalidation as possible. Mid-range entries force wider stops and worse R:R, so he waits. (S3, S2)

## 6. Stop loss placement

42. **The stop goes at the invalidation level, not wherever makes it smallest.** It sits below the low that should hold, leaving room for the initial low to be swept. "You put your stop where you are wrong on your idea." For a sweep-and-reclaim, the stop goes below the swept lows, and **a little further below, not directly under them**, "because you can still run multiple times". (S3, S13, V1)
43. **He often uses mental or conditional stops:** "cut on a clean break below X", or "cut if a 4h/daily closes back below the level". Position boxes still show an approximate stop. (S7, S8, S11, S12)
44. **Two versions of every call:** a **HTF version with a wider stop** for followers who sleep or work, and his own **LTF entry with a tight stop**. (S4)
45. **Typical observed stop distances** (from the 4 Oct boxes): BTC about 1–2%, HYPE about 2.6% (up to about 10% for deep zones), ZEC about 5%. Small caps need 10–20%, which is why they get little or no leverage. (S3, S15)

## 7. Position sizing, risk % and leverage

46. **"Leverage doesn't matter, position size does."** Leverage is just a by-product. He demonstrated on Blofin that the slider value is irrelevant. (S1, S3, S6)
47. **Formula:** position size = (account × risk %) ÷ stop distance %. Leverage = position size ÷ margin.
    - $1,000 account, risk 50% ($500), stop 10%: $5,000 position, 5x. (S1)
    - Stop 1%, risk 25%: 25x. Stop 1%, risk 50%: 50x. A 2% stop needs half the leverage. (S4)
    - About $2k account, risk 10%, stop 1%: $20k position, about 10x. (S12)
    - HYPE main zone on a $2.7k account: "20–50k position size", roughly 7–18x. (S13)
48a. **His official rule (pinned in the Telegram group):**
    - Risk is 20–50% of the account per trade, depending on the trade.
    - High caps like BTC get more risk, because more size goes in without moving the entry or exit. Lower caps get less, because liquidity is the constraint.
    - This is "NOT normal risk management and NOT how I would manage a large portfolio". The point of this stage is to compound $1k to $10k aggressively, and he is "completely comfortable" losing 20–50% on a single trade.
    - Once the account grows, risk comes down a lot, and the goal moves to protecting and compounding.
    - Last line: **"the trade is the same, the amount of your account you are willing to lose is completely different"**. Followers should take the same entry, stop and target, but choose their own risk %.
    - The pinned message gives no formula, leverage figure or stop rule. Those come only from the streams (points 47–50).
48. **Within that 20–50% range, risk scales with conviction** (from the streams):
    - **BTC S-tier: "near 40–50%"** ("I'm BissBTC, I don't miss on Bitcoin"). (S4)
    - Planned, long-awaited HYPE-type setup: about 40%, and it can be stacked as multiple entries. (S3)
    - ETH HTF S-tier: 40–50%. (S6)
    - Minor higher-low or LTF setups: 10–20%. Weaker scalps: about 5%. (S9)
    - **Lower timeframe = less risk. Higher timeframe = more risk.** A broken HTF level is a clean invalidation. An LTF trade can be "right direction, too early". (S12)
    - He also sizes on whether a failure could be recovered at the next zone. (S12)
49. **Observed versus stated:** in the first month he admitted "I've only been risking 5 to 10% on these trades" (S9), because he never got his pristine entry and had to take breakouts. The 2x–3x so far came from a few moderate-risk trades, not from 40–50% bets. He keeps saying the next pristine setup gets the big risk (S11, S14).
50. **Leverage by asset:** liquid and mature coins (BTC, then HYPE) get the highest leverage because the stops are tight and he "trusts the price action". Volatile small caps get low or no leverage ("10x is 2% from liquidation", "chop liquidates you before the move"). (S3, S10, S13)
51. **Scaling plan (phases):** 1k→10k is maximum risk, 10k→100k medium, 100k→1M low. The aim is to "move away from $1,000 as fast as possible", so he's then risking pure profit. Above 10k, risk drops a lot. (S1)
52. **He accepts blow-up risk at this stage:** "You have to be open to blowing your account at any moment and not be mad about it." (In 2020 he took $3k to $500k in three months, with excessive risk.) (S10)

## 8. Trade management and exits

53. **Take profit is always adjustable.** Targets are the next liquidity pool, range high or low, prior S/R, or the yearly or weekly open. A "set and forget" TP exists for followers. Live, he exits earlier or later as "new information every second" arrives. (S7, S11, S14)
54. **Partials:** he takes some at TP1 (key swing low or high), then lets the rest run to TP2 and TP3. In the challenge he often **closed all of it early** "so I don't give back profits publicly". He later admitted that cost him multiples (S6, S8, S9).
55. **Moving the stop to breakeven:**
    - On a planned HTF trade, BE "for peace of mind" is fear and costs you the move. There must be a reason. (S3)
    - On an LTF trade after TP1, or before sleeping, BE is fine. (S7)
    - On the public challenge he uses BE more ("I don't want to lose publicly"). He then re-enters on strength if price reclaims. (S8, S12)
56. **Trailing:** only with a reason, e.g. below each new higher low in a clear trend. Otherwise he prefers trimming at targets and rotating back in. (S3)
57. **Pyramiding (his ideal on big legs):**
    - Add on each pullback that reclaims, reinvesting profit. (S8)
    - Variant: after the first position breaks out of the range, move its stop to BE and add a second position risking the same $. That gives more size for the same risk. (S11)
    - He regrets not doing it on the Aug BTC breakout. When he did it on HYPE plus ETH (Sep), he gave profits back. (S9, S13)
58. **After a big win he stops.** He takes the evening or weekend off to avoid overtrading. (S6, S10)
59. **If the trade starts rejecting** (support turning into resistance), he just cuts it. There's no obligation to wait for TP or SL. (S12)

## 9. Shorts

60. **He prefers longs.** Shorts only when they're "blaringly obvious": **"staircase up, elevator down"**, a deviation above resistance that gets back below, or a yearly-open retest. Never "angrily shorting a god candle". (S5, S9, S10, S14)
61. Shorts get **less size** and usually target the range low or the liquidity below. On 4 Oct the short was marked "maybe", meaning lower conviction than the trend-side longs. (S12, S15)

## 10. What he avoids

62. Forcing trades out of boredom. Chasing after a 30% move. Buying the middle of a range. Weekend moves. Dead or illiquid coins. High leverage on small caps. On-chain or meme trading for the challenge. Copy trading (only for a $100k+ fee). ICT-style jargon. Moving the stop to BE out of fear. Taking every posted setup at once ("you'd stack −150%"). Changing the plan mid-trade because of P&L. (S1–S14)

## 14. A checklist to frame a trade "Biss-style"

1. **HTF context:** trend or range? Where's the range high, low and mid? Any yearly or weekly open nearby?
2. **Liquidity:** where are the biggest liquidation clusters (Coinglass 1w, 1M and 3M)? Which side hasn't been taken yet?
3. **Volatility:** is BVOL compressed? Is a quarterly or yearly candle close nearby (expect chop)? Is it a weekend?
4. **Who's trapped?** Late longs after a pump? Breakdown shorts under support?
5. **Zone:** an extreme only (range low or high, HTF S/R flip, cluster). Never mid-range.
6. **Trigger:** sweep, then reclaim (LTF close back inside or above). For a breakout, it must come after accumulation.
7. **Invalidation:** where's the idea wrong? Put the stop there, and decide whether it's a hard stop or "cut on a clean close below".
8. **Size:** risk % by tier (S-tier BTC 40–50% stated; LTF 5–20%), then size = risk$ ÷ stop%. Leverage follows from that. Majors only for high leverage.
9. **Plan the exit:** TP1 at the nearest liquidity or S/R, TP2 and TP3 at the range extreme or HTF level. Decide in advance whether to go BE (LTF) or hold (HTF).
10. **One position.** After a big win, step away.

## 15. His own 5-step system (V1, 7 Oct 2026 "Quick Course" video)

This is how he summarises his method for beginners. It confirms sections 3–6 and doesn't add new rules.

| Step | What he teaches | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Support & resistance | Support is where price stops falling and creates a swing low. Resistance is where price stops rising and creates a swing high. Broken resistance becomes support, and broken support becomes resistance. | **Validation: price pushed away in time and distance.** Beginners should mark zones; with experience, single lines. |
| 2. Market condition | There are only two: **range** (long support, short resistance, repeat until it breaks) and **trend** (HH/HL up, LH/LL down). | Price cycles range → trend → range → trend. Ranges also appear inside trends as consolidations. "We have a strategy for every scenario." |
| 3. Top-down analysis (**he calls this the most important step**) | Weekly/monthly first, then daily, then 4h/1h/15m for the entry. | HTF levels give the biggest reactions. Entering on the LTF at an HTF level gives a tighter stop and a better R:R than entering on the daily. If you skip this you end up shorting into HTF support. |
| 4. Liquidity | Obvious lows and highs hold everyone's stops. Price sweeps them, takes traders out, then moves the other way. | **Two traps:** (a) longs stopped out just under obvious support; (b) breakdown shorts who must buy back when price reclaims the level, which adds fuel to the move. He says he can tell in advance which levels will fake out, but only teaches that on streams or 1:1. |
| 5. Execution | **Entry:** once price is back above the support after sweeping below it. **SL:** below the swept lows, with extra room. **TP:** the other side of the range. | Worked example at 3R (risk 1 to make 3). A second entry is possible if it sweeps again and reclaims. |

**Worked example he uses: the BTC $58k bottom.**
- **Weekly:** the 2021 resistance flipped to support and was retested at about 58k.
- **Daily:** a support was found, then a second one broke below.
- **4h:** price swept the obvious lows (taking stops) and got back inside. He longed there with the stop below the lows and the target at the other side of the range.
- He says this produced "multiple six-figure days" on the move up (about +50%), and he was still in that BTC long as of 7 Oct.

Other notes from V1:
- **Tools:** TradingView for analysis, Blofin for execution.
- **Candles:** he often uses black and white candles, where white is bullish and black is bearish.
- **Shorting explained:** you profit if you buy back lower than you sold.
- **Marketing:** the last ~3 minutes pitch the 1:1 "inner circle" with student testimonials.

## 16. Honest notes and contradictions to keep in mind

- **Stated versus actual risk:** the pinned Telegram rule says 20–50% per trade, but on stream (S9) he admitted only 5–10% in the first month. The big-risk trade is always "the next one". The trade log suggests no single trade has risked 20–50% yet. Treat the 20–50% as a ceiling he permits himself, not the risk he has actually been taking.
- **"Never miss" and "not one loser":** his small loss (~$50 ETH) and round trips back to breakeven (BTC, HYPE) don't show in that claim. He also closes winners early, which flatters the win rate.
- **Order type** changed from "always market" to "limit at the zone, spread limits".
- **R-multiples:** on stream (S6) he mocked people who talk in "7R" and "2R", but his course video (V1) teaches setups as "a 3R trade".
- **BE stops:** he criticises fear-based BE, yet uses BE a lot on the public challenge.
- **Many calls go out but few are taken.** He posts many setups across BTC, ETH, HYPE, SOL, VVV, SPX, SpaceX and others, then counts all the winners. The challenge balance reflects only the ones he took.
- He promotes Blofin through an affiliate link and sells mentorship ("inner circle"), so streams also serve as marketing.
